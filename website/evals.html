Evaluation

Finding out which part of the policy failed

The policies we train have two levels. A high-level model (Molmo 2) looks at the camera and picks the next skill and a target point; a low-level model (ManiFlow) moves the arm. When an episode fails, we want to know which of the two was responsible, so the eval system lets you swap either one for an oracle.

Modes

A mode is a choice of high level and low level. All of them are started with scripts/launch_eval.py.

There is no mode with both oracles: that is just data generation. The low-level oracle always queries Molmo 2 when a skill ends, so it has no every-chunk variant. Running --policy maniflow --no-use-oracle gives ManiFlow only the episode's goal sentence, with no high level at all.

Rebuilding the scene

Every eval episode starts from an episode in a dataset. An episode queue reads the robot pose, block poses and sizes, table height and goal from the recording, and the policy has to reach the goal from there. Since the scene comes straight from the dataset, a difference in success rate between two models reflects the models and not a difference in setup.

The queue hands episodes out to GPU-parallel environments, so the same command works on a single workstation GPU or across a multi-GPU machine.

Generalization splits

Held-out tests are just different datasets. For example, train with blocks spawning only on the left side of the table and evaluate on the right, or train on stacks of 2 and 5 blocks and evaluate on 3 and 4.

Other options

These work on top of the modes above:

  • --oracle-comparison runs the symbolic planner alongside Molmo 2 and puts both in the overlay video (Molmo 2 modes only).
  • --init-after-hl-action flip,rotate starts each episode right after a given skill, which is handy for testing just the part of a task that comes next.
  • --use-render-params reproduces each rendered episode's camera, HDRI, table material, lights and exposure. --wave-resets groups episodes so each batch shares one look. Both need --no-use-calibrated-cameras.
  • --use-calibrated-cameras is on by default and gives every environment the real cameras' intrinsics.
  • --episode-end chooses what ends an episode: the goal, the time limit, or a play-mode low level saying it's done.
  • --replicate-noise starts every batched ManiFlow sample from the same noise, so identical observations give identical actions.

TAMPER: training on your failures

TAMPER (TAMP Expert Relabeling) is a DAgger-style loop with a planning expert. An eval run finds the states where the policy goes wrong; the expert then plans new demonstrations starting from those states. The next round of training data then covers situations the policy actually ends up in, not only the ones data generation happens to sample.

  1. Evaluate with capture on. Add --tamper_output_dir to an evaluate.py run. The final scene of every failed or timed-out episode is saved as a new environment package.
  2. Look at what was captured. tamper_failures.json lists each state with its episode, outcome and goal.
  3. Relabel. vecgen plans from the captured scene toward the recorded goal and executes it with the usual verified skills.
  4. Retrain and evaluate again. Annotate and merge the new episodes, add them to the training mix, and repeat.

In a small test on one RTX 5090, a random-action eval on a 16-episode stacking set captured 16 states, and vecgen recovered 12–13 of them in about 72 seconds.

Guide: docs/tamper.md

Outputs

Each run scores every episode against its goal and writes per-step metrics and run summaries. Full rollouts, videos included, are recorded as a LeRobot dataset by default, in the same format as the training data. Any policy that can be served over a WebSocket can be evaluated by implementing a small policy-server interface.
