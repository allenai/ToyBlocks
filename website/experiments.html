Experiments

What we've found so far

A first set of experiments run with ToyBlocks: how well learned policies generalize, how a high-level model copes with new blocks, and how frontier models plan when they act through skill tools. All numbers here come from the blog post.

Low-level models in new places

We trained low-level action models in one part of the task space and tested them in another: blocks only on the left half of the table during training and on the right half at test time, or stacks of 2 and 5 blocks in training and stacks of 3 and 4 at test time. Despite large-scale pretraining, they generalized poorly at first: 5% on the unseen half of the table, and 29% on unseen stack heights. Each row shows one technique added on its own. With all of them combined, success reaches 81% and 89%, close to the in-domain numbers.

Molmo 2 on new blocks

Molmo 2 runs as the high level, with either the motion-planned skills (the low-level oracle) or ManiFlow as the low level. Seen uses the training blocks in new arrangements, and unseen uses held-out blocks. Easy tasks are stacking only, medium tasks add re-orienting blocks, and hard tasks also need searching for hidden letters.

Frontier-model agents

With the Agentic Framework, a frontier model (Claude Opus 5.5 in these runs) sees the camera images and calls ToyBlocks skills as MCP tools, pointing at the block it wants. It gets no success signal, so it has to decide for itself when the task is done, or when it can't be done.

30 / 30of the same tasks solved closed loop, choosing each action from camera images
63%of search-and-spell tasks solved, or correctly declared impossible

Search and spell

The agent has to stack a three-letter word, finding the letters among up to eight blocks. In half of the scenes a letter is missing, and then the right answer is to say the task is impossible. On patterned blocks, each block carries one letter in several forms (upper and lower case, plus pictures that start with it), so the agent can work out what a hidden face must show. On unpatterned blocks the faces are unrelated. With patterns the agent finishes having turned over fewer faces, and on infeasible scenes it declares the task impossible much sooner.

Learning its own skill descriptions

When a skill behaves unexpectedly, the agent forms a hypothesis, runs a small test and revises its understanding, then writes its own descriptions of the skills. Those learned descriptions work better than the ones an expert wrote.

On a real robot

Trained only on ToyBlocks data, ManiFlow's single skills succeeded about as often on a real Franka FR3 as in simulation (81.1% against 80.0%). Molmo 2 + ManiFlow spelled three-letter words in 15 of 30 real trials, against 55.5% in simulation.
